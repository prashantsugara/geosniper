# Duel multiplayer

The current build supports AI practice and direct LAN 1v1. Both players need this build (protocol v6). On the same Wi-Fi/hotspot, open Sniper Duel, host a room and choose **My live location** or **Choose a place**, then join its LAN code/IP on the other device. UDP port 7777 is gameplay; 7776 is LAN discovery. Internet Relay is not yet configured.

The host resolves fresh GPS (with location permission) or a manually searched place, loads its geographic map and sends a compressed snapshot of all feature outlines, attributes, water rings and terrain heights. The guest builds that snapshot without requesting its own GPS or downloading a different map. Reliable 900-byte chunks are sent in bounded windows; each room clears its snapshot when closed. Sector streaming is disabled for duels so independently downloaded geometry cannot diverge. Location loading can be cancelled; multiplayer never switches only one peer to the old offline arena. Offline bot practice remains separate.

The host selects safe perches in the shared sector and sends them to the guest. A ready/engage exchange gates each round and allows the guest to finish generating its world. Weapon queries recognize the rival avatar; the host decides damage and round scores. Fire requests, confirmed results, scores, perches, readiness and rematches are retried, ordered and deduplicated. Round identifiers reject stale shots and results. A round death does not enter the solo mission-failure screen, and each round replenishes ammo. A rematch requires both players to agree; completed scores stay visible until the host confirms the new match. Requests can be declined or cancelled and expire after 20 seconds. Leaving or reconnecting cancels a pending request.

Settings → Touch & Gyro Aiming → Customize Touch Controls opens a safe-area preview. Drag buttons and the joystick, adjust size and opacity, choose the left-handed preset, or reset defaults. Save applies the profile; Cancel preserves the previous profile. Positions adapt to screen size and safe-area changes, and the same rectangles drive drawing and touch input. Settings also offers Clean or Detailed combat HUD modes. Clean keeps health, ammo, objectives, rival guidance and ballistic essentials visible, while Detailed adds technical readouts.

This rematch protocol uses version 7. Rebuild and install the same version on both peers. Run `Tools/run_duel_visual_checks.ps1 -ExecuteMethod MultiplayerPolishChecks.RunAll` for the isolated Unity control-layout, HUD-layout, rematch, gameplay and model-render checks. Physical multitouch and two-device rematch timing still need Android testing.

Legacy X- endpoint codes can be decoded but are no longer advertised by an unrelated STUN socket. They do not provide NAT traversal. Shots are resolved by the host; a trusted host can still alter the match, so this is not dedicated-server anti-cheat.

## Unity internet multiplayer

Use Unity Multiplayer Services SDK sessions with Relay and Netcode for GameObjects/Unity Transport for the internet version. Relay replaces direct UDP room hosting and handles internet connectivity. Move round state, health and shot validation to the host/server using reliable messages/RPCs, while movement and aim use an appropriate state-sync channel. Preserve AI practice separately.

This project currently has no Multiplayer Services, Netcode or Transport package configured. Cloud integration requires linking the Unity project to a UGS project/environment and enabling Authentication/Relay. No Unity cloud configuration or account changes were made in this fix.

## Host shooting, recovery and last moments

Protocol v6 routes duel damage through host validation of Fire requests. Fixed per-round rifle magazines, damage, scope presets and cadence replace progression upgrades; all three rifles are available in PvP. The host checks round and shot identity, finite ray data, origin near the known eye position, ammunition and timed reloads. It intersects calibrated head/body volumes against a bounded pose history, then checks the shared world for solid cover or up to four thin penetrable surfaces. Guest shots rewind host pose history by measured RTT up to 250ms; stale poses older than 250ms reject damage. Multiplayer uses consistent hitscan; solo projectile ballistics are unchanged. Host-confirmed results alone apply health, hit markers and deaths, with reliable ordering and deduplication. Legacy peer Hit payloads and guest-reported health cannot decide damage.

This is host authority with client-reported movement and bounded shot timestamps, not a complete anti-cheat service. Moving cover is checked at its current host position rather than rewound. The host remains trusted. Hardware latency, input feel and real phone packet loss still need playtesting.

After eight seconds of gameplay silence, both peers pause through an authenticated reconnect exchange for up to 30 seconds. Map data, rules, arena, health, scores, ammunition and current round remain in memory. Round/countdown and weapon timers stop while reconnecting. The reconnect handshake consumes and discards pre-pause fire/reload requests by reliable packet ID, while already-confirmed damage/results retain delivery. A fresh reliable host snapshot restores the guest shot clock, all three magazines, active reload and remaining chamber cooldown before allowing new fire. Session tokens reject traffic from prior rooms and allow an authenticated guest source-port change. Tokens do not encrypt UDP traffic. An explicit leave, recovery deadline or application process restart ends the session; restoring a killed app and reaching a changed host IP are not supported.

Confirmed elimination shows a 2.2-second, skippable replay of the victim's previous two seconds, labeled LAST MOMENTS / YOUR VIEW. The recap names the killer, rifle, hit zone, applied final-hit damage and range. It records the already-rendered screen, including scope/HUD, at 8fps with 17 GPU frames capped at 384x216 (about 5.7MB), plus a temporary screen-sized capture buffer (about 8MB at 1080p). No gameplay actors or camera transforms are replayed. Frames are released on round changes, reconnect, leave and skip. Screen capture and its performance still require Android testing.

Run `Tests/DuelShotChecks/DuelShotChecks.csproj` for the production validator/history geometry, cadence and reload checks. Transport checks include real UDP reconnect, acknowledgment loss, exactly-once ordered delivery, preserved room snapshots, stale session rejection and endpoint migration. Destructible prop explosions are disabled for multiplayer shots until they can be authored and replicated by the host; otherwise local explosions could bypass synchronized damage. Solo props retain their existing behavior.
